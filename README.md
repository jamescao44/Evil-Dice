# Evil Dice

A Godot 4 pixel-art dice game: grow an eldritch horror through **nineteen squad waves → castle-city fever**, earning treasure through Perfect throws.

The shared [Chinese Google Design Doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1i-PEouZmzwagpz6CTa7CjMVl_UJd99-io_uB7TMgguM/edit) is the primary record for game designs and changes. Write future design updates there in Mandarin (Simplified Chinese), keeping proposals separate from implemented features. The earlier English Google Doc and `docs/game-design.md` remain reference snapshots.

## Language

The interface defaults to 简体中文. Open **Settings** on the home screen, or the three-bar menu in battle, to choose **简体中文** or **English**. Changes apply immediately and are saved locally, including browser storage in web builds.

Translations live in `assets/localization/ui.csv`. During local play in Godot, saving the CSV updates the running game in about a second—no manual reimport or restart. Keep the `keys` column and formatting placeholders unchanged. Invalid or incomplete saves keep the last valid translations and report the problem in Godot's output. Exported builds still bundle translations at build time; updating a published web game requires a new export/upload. See `docs/code-architecture.md` for implementation details.

## Play

Open `project.godot` in Godot and click **Run Project** for the portrait home screen. Open `scenes/home.tscn` and use **Run Current Scene** for the landscape home screen. The original `scenes/battle.tscn` and `scenes/battle_portrait.tscn` remain available for direct battle testing. Gameplay uses clicks or taps, with no keyboard controls.

The home screen has five bottom tabs, ordered **Dices / Ancients / Play / Summon / Shop**, with Play selected initially. Ancients shows The Horror, its lore and play style, and a skill reference for normal rolls, all doubles and all triples, including city variants. Swipe its portrait horizontally, drag with a mouse, or open the visual selection menu to browse the collection. Only The Horror is available; the carousel stays on it until more entries exist. Dices contains the basic Bone Dice, with a larger preview and descriptions on tap. The Play button displays the selected dice and starts a new twenty-stage run in the same layout. Home in the battle grimoire or ending screen leaves the run and returns to Play, retaining the current session's selections and sound/effects/speed settings. Summon is a placeholder; Shop is blank. Soul Crystals and summon shards exist only within the active run; there is no summoning, purchasing, or saved collection progression yet.

1. Click or tap inside the circular magic seal to start charging, then again to lock the marker and throw. The marker moves fastest near the center, using the gentler blended timing curve at another **10% slower overall speed** (about 2.667 seconds per round trip). The visible bar is 25% shorter: 216 landscape / 432 portrait logical pixels. Higher power increases combination odds and dice motion and sound. A **Perfect** within **±30 ms** of crossing the center, measured from the actual stop-input time guarantees a pair or triple: 50% each in squads, 100% triple in fever. Non-Perfect triples cap at 25% in squads; exact doubles cap at 70% in both modes after fever normalization. There are no pity guarantees. The marker moves continuously while charging; only a successful Perfect stop snaps it to the center and flashes the marker and bar white. Both layouts share the same time window regardless of frame rate.
2. Revealed dice form a **skill bubble**, rise enlarged to the middle, then settle lower and bob with their name and description. The used dice seal turns gray and its combination lines disappear. Dice remain visible and dim through held skills, casts, enemy turns and transitions, then brighten when ready; menus dim them further instead of hiding them. **In squad waves, drag skills onto the battlefield to cast.** Normal attacks, 11 and 111 must land on a foe, or can be tapped first and then aimed by tapping a foe. City rolls cast automatically after their reveal; saved targeted skills still select the city. Buffs, area skills and combos do not cast on a simple tap. Dragging a buff dims enemies while the Horror stays bright; attack selection and every cast keep characters fully lit. Dragging has no tether line or cancel hint; casting bubbles become see-through at 28% opacity with no dark fill. Invalid drops preserve the skill. **55 automatically delivers three hits; 555 delivers six after one field drop.** Each hit pops a dot and independently chooses a random living foe (the city in fever); remaining hits continue against bodies after all living foes are defeated. Cast bubbles stay where released and dissolve into pixel particles when finished. Enemy turns, boon clocks, the next roll and treasure claims wait for the whole action.
3. Every living enemy acts. Before acting, enemies show a sword (attack), plus (heal), shield, or boost-arrow intent icon. Hold an enemy for 0.25 seconds to inspect its move, buffs and status effects while the rest of the screen dims; release to dismiss. A short tap still casts a selected targeted skill, while holding never spends it. Dead bodies stay until the whole squad is defeated.
4. The Horror feeds, the bodies fade, and the scenery pans and shrinks as you advance and grow. Adventurers become progressively smaller across nineteen squad waves, leading into the city finale.
5. Stage twenty is a circular castle city with a moat, bridges, dense houses, and a towering keep. Animation speed is fixed at 2×, with no frenzy bar or hold/spam speed boost. Pair and triple probabilities receive a 1.5× fever boost (non-Perfect triple ceiling 37.5%); if their total exceeds 100%, both are normalized proportionally. Perfect guarantees a triple. Every throw still needs a charge click and a stop click; holding never automatically releases dice. Each newly rolled skill automatically casts when its bubble finishes forming, including support and all combo hits; no aiming or field drop is needed. Rolls are still started and stopped manually, and saved bonus skills remain manual. Troops keep attacking while you charge and during animations outside cast pauses. They wiggle as they run, and civilians and aristocrats flee from destroyed houses inside the walls.

Top controls: ordinary animation speed, sound, and a **three-bar menu**. The menu page contains **Debug**, **Effects: Full/Reduced**, **Help / Grimoire**, and **Resume**; Help and Debug return to this page when closed. Active statuses appear as small independent icons directly above the Horror, each with its own remaining attack casts, turns, burning stacks, next-throw stun count, or city-immunity seconds. Inactive or expired effects disappear individually. Both attack boons, invincibility, freeze, burning, stun, darkness, corrosion, and invisibility use separate indicators; both attack boons add their bonuses while active. Hover for explanations or tap in portrait. Help includes a dedicated status page with concise descriptions of all eight combat statuses. The menu page, Help and Debug pause the charge meter, city attacks, charging audio and skill effects. Losing window focus cancels an unfinished charge without spending a roll. Restart after victory or defeat begins a new run.

The **SAVE slot** above and to the right of the dice stores one skill. Dragging a new skill into the empty slot ends the current turn without applying it. Later, drag the stored skill out for a bonus cast between rolls or while holding a new skill; targeted stored skills also support tap-then-select. The saved seal expands to full active size. Any waiting skill stays visible but dimmed and unavailable until the bonus finishes. Bonus casts do not add an enemy phase or tick existing boon durations; the subsequent ordinary action still does. A saved one-turn immunity protects the upcoming squad enemy phase. If a bonus cast clears the wave, an unused summoned skill automatically fills the vacated slot. Saved skills carry between waves, use current damage stats when cast, and reset with a new run. A full slot rejects replacement. The saved skill stays motionless and dimmed whenever interaction is locked, then resumes movement when usable.

## Combat and growth

Every rolled or saved skill is a `MultiHitSkill` object. Normal attacks, aimed attacks, area attacks, support and ultimates start with **one use**, hiding the use orbs; 55 starts with three and 555 with six. Future use multipliers apply to the entire skill: **×2 makes a regular skill two uses/two orbs, and Sixfold Ruin twelve hits/twelve orbs**. Damage previews, orb pops and combat all use the same count. Aimed skills keep their chosen living target and retarget after a kill; random combos choose independently each hit. Area and support skills repeat their whole effect, retaining the existing buff-refresh and healing limits. The full chain remains one action; it spends remaining hits on bodies before victory, and stops on player defeat.

For future modifiers, call `model.set_skill_use_multiplier("echo_buff", 2)` for all skills, or `skill.set_use_multiplier("relic_echo", 2)` for one skill object. Passing `1` removes that named source; refreshing the same source replaces it, while different sources multiply together. Saved skills preserve their own modifiers and use current model modifiers when cast. The use budget freezes at cast start so an expiring modifier cannot change the remaining hits or orb count midway. New runs clear model modifiers. Twin Scythes and Cursed Bow supply ordered combo-use modifiers through this shared budget. For each wave cleared while owned, Cursed Bow adds one combo hit and compounds single-target damage by ×0.95 (`0.95^waves`); it has no penalty before the first clear and does not penalize area attacks.

Normal base damage is **1.5 × sum of three different dice + flat relic damage**, with the existing normal per-hit caps. Skills use **regular attack power P = 15 + flat relic damage** and ignore caps. Let `s` be the spare die. Every numeric player hit is **floor(base damage × DMG)**, then normal caps, shields and remaining HP apply. **DMG starts at 1 + active boon bonuses + 0.1 × Perfect combo**, then applies relic modifiers in inventory order, followed by darkness. The natural baseline remains **1.0 in every wave, including the city**. Might adds +0.5, and Ascension adds +2.0; both active boons add together. The white damage multiplier at the battlefield’s top left stays hidden until a numerical attack is cast. It starts at `DMG (regular base) x 1.0`, changes to the skill base, applies bonuses and relics left to right, then combines into final damage. The entire expression waves; damage above 100 trembles with log10 scaling. It fades after the attack. Relics glow and tilt as larger, shiny versions of the multi-hit orbs strike the number; increases bob in a wave and decreases shake. Criticals are included in the cast display; flat-base and extra-hit relics react without changing DMG. Support skills and instant kills keep it hidden. Reduced effects keep the values and glow but suppress motion and particles. The former 1.5× starting multiplier is baked into base damage, including spare-die contributions; multiplier bonuses now apply relative to the 1.0 starting baseline. City 666 uses its explicit 2000 base instead. Keep fractional bases until the final hit is rounded down.

| Face | Double | Triple |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | One hit: 1.5P + 1.5s | Instantly kill one chosen foe; city: 5P base damage |
| 2 | Apply 1 Frozen stack to every enemy; non-attack, city immune | Restore full health, including grown maximum HP |
| 3 | +0.5 DMG for the next s attack casts | +2.0 DMG for the next 3 attack casts |
| 4 | Hit every foe for ⅔P + 1.5s | Hit every foe for 2P |
| 5 | Three automatic hits, each P + 1.5s; independently random foes | Six automatic hits, each P; independently random foes |
| 6 | Invincible for s enemy phases; city: 2 seconds per stack | **Annihilation**: clear squad; city: clear troops and deal 2000 × DMG. Heal 12 |

Both attack boons add their +0.5 and +2.0 bonuses to DMG. They consume **one charge per completed attack cast**, covering all hits; saved attacks also consume one charge. Support skills, banking, skipped turns, tarot damage, relic procs and enemy phases do not consume them. Charges carry between waves and follow the same rule in City Fever. Reapplying the same attack boon emits a 10-base (33) or 50-base (333) shockwave using current modifiers, then refreshes its charges. Double-2 is now **Abyssal Frost**: one additive Frozen stack per living enemy, no damage or attack triggers. Strength increases the applied stacks; the city is immune. Triple-2 remains a full heal. Invincibility adds its new duration to the time remaining and counts down after the last enemy's action. In the city, immunity lasts **2 real-time seconds per stack**, including idle, charging and casting; menus/rewards pause it. Reapplying preserves a partially elapsed stack. Bonus casts do not advance turn clocks. Multi-hit actions finish their remaining hits against corpses after the last living foe dies. The Chariot deals **50 fixed damage** to each enemy (or the city), unaffected by damage bonuses or damage multipliers, while respecting shields and retaining attack-relic effects.

Held attack skills show live per-hit damage as **base + modifier (xN)**; targeted skills show it when selected or dragged. For example, a 5/5/1 without bonuses reads `16 + 0 (x3)`. The modifier includes the global multiplier, relics, attack boons, Perfect combo, critical charges, darkness, corrosion and normal target caps. Base is the integer part of the skill base; the final sum matches combat's rounded impact damage before squad shields and remaining HP. Hovering a target selects its cap; before choosing, differing caps show a modifier range. Cap reductions can produce a negative modifier. Saved previews use current stats. This is an extra red line above the bubble; the original attack description stays below it. Squad instant kills have no numeric line; city 111 and 666 remain numerical. Freeze has no numeric damage/healing line; full heal shows the actual missing health as green `+N HP`. Portrait skills settle slightly lower to leave room between this line and the treasure bank. Attack-buff captions use “for N attacks”; invincibility uses “for N turns” or city “for N seconds”. An active matching boon adds a short gold reapply-bonus line beneath the regular skill description.

After each squad victory, gain 30 maximum HP and heal 55 up to the new maximum. There is no automatic damage-multiplier increase between waves. Normal caps are ⅓ HP in stages 1–2, ½ in stage 3, and ¾ from stage 4 onward. Growth is fixed, with no mutation choices. The Horror grows steadily from 224×224 on wave 1 to 288×288 on wave 20. Enemy display size decreases steadily from 192 on wave 1 to 44 on wave 19 (the former wave 9 size); city troops remain 20. Paving shrinks from 96 to 16 and scenery from 128 to 16 over the nineteen squad waves; city scenery finishes at 12. During the 1.65-second growth transition, the feet stay planted as the Horror stretches up, compresses, swells slightly beyond its new size, then settles exactly. Reduced effects keeps the steady size increase without the bounce. Enemies form compact groups through the battlefield: landscape groups shift inward, while portrait groups use the upper center and open right flank, reserving room for the Horror and status icons. Health bars, intent icons and target areas remain separate.

The city has 20,000 HP and the same 1.0 damage baseline as squad waves. **666 deals 2000 × the full DMG multiplier**, limited by remaining city HP, using the same multiplier as other numeric attacks. At ×1.0, city 666 deals 2000 damage before flat relic bonuses. City 111 deals 5× regular attack power, not an instant city kill. AoE clears all troops; each rapid hit removes at most one troop. Heal and power-up triples do no damage and do not clear troops. Invincibility blocks both troop and ranged damage; city timers and reinforcements continue. Buildings and walls turn to rubble as health falls.

## Perfect combo and treasure

A **Perfect combo** counter sits opposite the saved skill slot, showing the consecutive Perfect count. The total damage multiplier is shown separately in white at the battlefield’s top left. Each accepted Perfect adds one Combo and **+0.1 to DMG**, without a cap; the bonus adds alongside treasure and both attack boons. The bonus applies to the skill from that throw and every subsequent hit, including saved skills at their current cast-time stats and city Annihilation. A non-Perfect throw resets it to zero. Canceling charge, using saved skills, claiming treasure, breaking ice, and changing waves preserve the streak; restarting clears it. The multiplier applies before integer rounding and normal damage caps. Healing and instant kills retain their existing behavior. Debug Perfects count once.

The treasure bank shows nine diamonds without a heading. Perfect throws fill **nine diamond slots**, preserved through misses and waves. The bank stops at nine until claimed. **3 Perfects = one reward; 6 = a rare chest with three rewards; 9 = an epic chest with five rewards.** This replaces the old random 25% three-reward bonus. The **third, sixth and ninth diamonds** are larger when filled. Only the highest available chest has a strong pulsing halo and a bright, clickable **CLAIM** label; lower tiers cannot be claimed. Claiming empties the entire meter, including extra diamonds. There is no separate chest button. Claims are disabled while a skill forms, waits, targets or casts, including pauses between multi-hit casts. The whole skill must finish before rewards can boost subsequent actions. Final city victory automatically claims the highest available chest and locks its one/three/five rewards. A brief “Common/Rare/Epic chest auto claimed” banner overlays the success screen for 2.4 real-time seconds. The normal chest-opening and reward-reel animation then plays automatically, and **each gift waits for the player to tap and collect it**. Rewards apply once on pickup; after the last gift’s effect, the success screen returns. Incomplete banks show no claim message. Squad waves preserve the bank; defeat retains manual claims.

The model selects every reward before animation: **50% relic**, **25% soul crystals**, and **25% summon shard**. A relic reward first rolls its rarity, then picks uniformly among eligible relics in that rarity. Wave 1 starts at **58% common / 30% rare / 10% epic / 1% legendary / 1% cursed**. Each subsequent wave moves every rate 10% of its remaining distance toward 20%: `20 + (initial − 20) × 0.9^(wave − 1)`. Wave 20 is approximately **25.13 / 21.35 / 18.65 / 17.43 / 17.43%**. Wave choices, treasure relics, Emperor and Fool share these rates; eligibility restrictions and pre-animation reward locking still apply. Cursed is not a separate reward category roll. Repeated categories are allowed, but concrete relic prizes are distinct, unowned and non-fusion. Empty rarity pools redistribute proportionally; if no eligible relics remain, the gift becomes soul crystals. Reels use separate cosmetic RNG and include every obtainable reward, scrolling actual relic icons and land on the already locked reward. A 0.38-second pause and white flash precede the chest opening and throwing loot up into extended slot reels. Five rewards use a 3+2 layout on phones and a row in landscape. **All rewards wait for individual taps after every reel stops, including the automatically claimed victory chest.** Each tap applies that gift once, shows its skill-style name, and plays its category effect. Tap anywhere during chest preparation or spinning to reveal all locked rewards immediately; that tap never claims or discards a gift. **SKIP REMAINING** becomes available after the reveal and after any number of pickups, including full-inventory replacement. It keeps collected gifts, discards the rest without compensation, and immediately returns to combat or the success screen. Combat and city timers pause while treasure is open. One relic reward slot becomes a tarot draw, using the same three-card selection as wave rewards. Soul Crystals rain; curses glow ominously; relics shine. Flat damage, multiplier upgrades, and potion placeholders no longer drop or appear in the reels. Reduced effects suppresses flash, shake, and particle bursts.

| Reward | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Soul Crystals | +100 run soul crystals; ruby icon and rain replace coins |
| Relic | Roll rarity first, then pick an eligible relic uniformly within it; includes cursed relics |
| Summon Shard | +1 run shard, represented by a parchment contract with a magic circle |

Soul Crystal and shard counters appear in the header. New runs clear both currencies, the Perfect bank, rewards, and bonuses; no persistent economy or summoning is implemented. Instant kills remain unscaled; city Annihilation uses the full DMG multiplier. Debug Perfects count.

Dice use weighted category and face selection. Triple faces 1–5 each have weight **1**, and 666 has weight **0.7** (about 12.28% of triple results). This preserves the total triple probability: regular Perfects remain 50% triple / 50% pair; city Perfects retain guaranteed triples. Non-Perfect caps remain 25% triples (up to 37.5% in fever) and 70% pairs. Per-run category, pair, triple, single-face, and reward weights remain explicit hooks. Lucky Die adds five percentage points to the chance of a roll containing a six; Cursed Die and Cursed Stopwatch modify accepted rolls in inventory order, with the stopwatch always forbidding 666. Zero-power category probabilities retain their old values, but triple faces are now weighted.


Damage popups show the calculated hit before the remaining-health clamp, including lethal hits, burning, and Prismatic strikes. Actual HP loss and lifesteal remain capped by remaining HP. Squad instant kills show impact effects without damage numbers; city 111 and 666 remain numeric attacks. Number size is `24 × max(1, log10(damage))`, rounded to a whole pixel size, so 10 / 100 / 1,000 damage use 24 / 48 / 72. Other feedback keeps its regular size.

## Tarot

The 22 tarot cards are single-use consumables in a separate, unlimited inventory with duplicates. Random choices and automatic grants use weight 1 per card, except The World at 0.5; bans renormalize the remaining pool. Automatic grants (generating relics, Empress and debug grants) briefly reveal the cards, then shrink them into the inventory icon in batches of up to three. Combat keeps running; settings, choices and other reward screens defer the animation. Hand-picked rewards keep their existing collection flight. Their effects can change rolls, react to roll outcomes, modify the next skill, or apply immediately. Tap the card icon to switch the dice area to the swipeable carousel; tap a card to inspect or drag it onto the battlefield to use it. The battlefield keeps running. Reward draws shuffle a deck from right to left, then fan it out; pick three, reveal them, and keep one. Tap anywhere during preparation to open the fan immediately, or during the three-card reveal to finish it. The skip tap is consumed. Draw the next card while earlier cards are still flying; there is no wait between picks. Drawn cards bob gently with staggered phases and remain still with reduced effects.

## Relics

Every cleared wave, including the city, offers two distinct relics and a tarot draw. Each relic choice rolls the current wave’s rarity rates (starting at **58% common / 30% rare / 10% epic / 1% legendary / 1% cursed** and gradually approaching 20% each), then picks uniformly within that rarity, excluding owned unique relics and fusion-only results. These are total probabilities per rarity, independent of the number of relics it contains. Empty rarity pools are removed and the remaining weights renormalized. Choose one, or skip for **+10 soul crystals**. A Game Coin held before clearing also grants +10 soul crystals. The offer locks until resolved and cannot be rerolled by reopening it.

The inventory holds up to ten relics outside the shortened battlefield, below the raised player health bar. Sourced fantasy sprites sit on one translucent rounded bar, without visible cells or slot numbers. Rarity glows appear beneath enlarged/held inventory items, reward choices, pickups and chest reels: white common, blue rare, bright purple epic, peach legendary and red cursed. Resting inventory items have no rarity glow; the slightly larger icons sit on a lower bar. Treasure reels use stronger rarity glows and colored frames; revealed relics also show their rarity label. During spinning, color follows the visible symbol without exposing the locked reward. Frames use the same rarity palette; magic stones retain their elemental sprite colors. Hold a relic to show its description, including counts and stored charges; releasing dismisses it. The held icon enlarges slightly, moves only horizontally, and a grey rounded square marks its destination while neighbors preview the order. Small bottom-right counters show page stacks, stored charges, Reaper kills, and Cursed Bow extra hits. Charged Magic Shield and Magic Sword repeat a gold claim-style glow until their last stored charge is spent; reduced effects keeps a steady glow. Effects resolve **left to right**. With ten occupied slots, selecting a new relic asks which existing relic to replace. Chest pickups use the same replacement flow, with an option to leave that gift behind (no skip soul crystals). Picking a relic plays a short lift-and-slam into its inventory position, with a bounce, impact ring and landing sound; stacks, replacements and fusions share the animation. Rewards commit once on selection, and progression waits for the landing. Codex Pages share one slot; the tenth fuses into Ancient Codex. All six magic stones fuse into Prismatic Stone at the earliest component position. Both fusion relics are excluded from drops, and their components stop appearing once the fused relic is owned. Inventory and relic counters reset on a new run.

All **53 relics** in the current design are implemented. Runestone is epic; Twin Scythes is legendary. Rarity rates move 10% of the remaining distance toward 20% each wave. Flat damage, health, armor, lifesteal, immunity, ordered damage modifiers, combo-hit modifiers and roll modifiers feed the shared combat model. Magic Shield and Magic Sword count six completed turns across waves, then store a whole-hit block or a ×3 critical for the next attack skill and all its hits. Saved bonus casts do not advance these counters. Last Contract prevents one death and restores 60% max HP. Reaper's Scythe counts enemy kills but excludes city troops. Prismatic Stone combines all six stones and adds a 100-base-damage strike on one random foe for each Perfect. Fortune Cookie grants a random tarot on acquisition and every three completed turns; Crystal Ball grants one each completed turn. Both respect tarot bans and carry counters across waves. Ancient Prophecy activates each used tarot twice, with fresh choices/targets when needed and the existing stacking/overwrite rules. The repeat cannot recursively repeat, even if Fool replaces the relic. A second impossible effect is skipped; the player may decline a repeat requiring input. Emperor/World resolve and lock each eligible reward before its picker.

The new relics add flat multiplier bonuses (Fries, Hamburger), extra combo hits (Worn/Royal Dagger), additive pair/triple odds (Four/Five-leaf Clover), roll-triggered attacks (Worn Longbow, Tissue Pack, Magic Slingshot, Ghost and Dragon), attack healing (Bat), status modifiers (Lizard, Ice Rose, Slime), and retaliation/Perfect attacks (Spiked Helmet). Item attacks use the common shield/status/damage-event paths without spending the pending skill, Strength, Priestess or attack-boon charges. Fixed damage ignores damage bonuses; base damage uses the current DMG formula. Ice Rose has a recursion guard.

Disguise Mask copies its immediate left neighbor except Ancient Tentacle. Tentacle repeats every relic to its left, including Mask: `item → Mask → Tentacle` activates the item four times. Passive bonuses and trigger payouts repeat; turn counters advance once. Copied activations, multiplier flights and queued roll attacks animate at 1.5× speed, multiplying the existing battle playback speed. Reordering never grants health. Cursed Dice cover all six faces, but only one may be held; owned/reserved family members are excluded from subsequent drops. Soul Vessel adds 15% to all Soul Crystal income per activation; fractional bonuses carry forward within the run. Ancient Prophecy copies each add one extra tarot activation, with the entire replay queue snapshotted to prevent recursion.


| Status | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Burning | 5 damage per stack after the enemy phase; capped at 10; loses 1 stack per turn |
| Freeze | Player breaks ice with 3 taps, ending the turn; enemies skip one action per freeze stack |
| Stun | Scrambles the next player throw; enemies cannot be stunned |
| Darkness | Halves outgoing damage and blocks healing; 1 stack by default unless an effect explicitly specifies more, loses 1 per turn |
| Corrosion | Disables defenses and increases incoming damage by 50%; 1 stack by default unless an effect explicitly specifies more, loses 1 per turn |
| Plague | Reduces max HP by 10 per stack to a minimum of 1; grows by 1 and spreads 1 stack to a random living, uninfected ally per turn; persists |

Tower applies one base stack per random application; Moon applies one permanent Darkness stack. Purple/Green Magic Stones, including Prismatic Stone, also apply one base stack. Explicit multi-stack skills remain supported. Runestone adds one application stack to subsequent status relics in inventory order. Enemy status icons appear below their health bars, enemy buffs (shield and next-attack boost) appear alongside ailments in hold inspection. Breaking a shield immobilizes its owner for one enemy action; corrosion or execution bypasses do not count as breaks. Freeze and shield-break locks overlap on the same skipped action. Plague spreads only to living allies without plague; newly infected allies neither receive a second spread nor spread it themselves that turn. Existing infections still gain one stack each turn. Player plague is disabled. Player statuses carry into the city, whose own health target remains immune.

## Twenty waves and weighted enemies

`wave_catalog.gd` defines the nineteen squads from the design table, followed by the **20,000-HP city** on wave 20. General slots randomly select Vanguard, Archer or Mage; fixed slots create Paladins and High Priests at the specified elite tier. All HP and attack stats stay at 1×. The Horror gains 30 base maximum HP per cleared squad, reaching 670 before relic modifiers at the city. Names remain hidden in battle; intent icons and hold descriptions show the locked next action.

| Enemy | Normal HP / attack | Elite HP / attack | Base pool (weight) | Added elite skills (weight) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vanguard | 75 / 5 | 150 / 10 | Attack (5), self-shield +5 / cap 20 (5) | Ally shield +5 (3), attack +1 stun (3) |
| Elf Archer | 50 / 7 | 100 / 10 | Attack (10), self-heal 30% (5) | Group heal 20% (3), attack +1 freeze (3) |
| Mage | 35 / 10 | 75 / 15 | Attack (10), bless ally or self ×1.5 next attack (10) | Fireball +1 burning (5) |
| Paladin | 500 / 20 | 1000 / 30 | Attack (3), self-shield +50 / cap 100 (3), charge (3), rest (10) | Self-shield +100 / cap 100 (3) |
| High Priest | 100 / 20 | 200 / 20 | Attack (3), group blessing including self (3), attack +2 random player-safe status (3) | Chant (3) |

Only valid skills enter each weighted draw: full health/shields and already-blessed targets cannot waste support choices. Freeze is excluded while the player is already frozen. Elemental status and support targets lock at round start. Healing cannot resurrect; blessing persists through support turns and applies across the next attack's full burst. All elites retain their initial 10 shield.

Paladins charge twice, then release two strikes with stun on the next turn. Elite High Priests chant three times, then release six strikes on the following turn. Charging becomes a committed sequence; freeze or immobilization cancels its progress and restarts from the first charge after recovery. Charge pips, a clock intent icon, hold descriptions and sequential impacts expose the sequence. Paladins and High Priests use distinct CC0 sprites, with gold and violet elite auras.

Living Paladins protect allies: targeted/random attacks and targeted tarot must select a Paladin. Area attacks still hit everyone, dealing full damage to Paladins and half to other enemies; area status effects still apply. **666 kills the entire squad as usual.** Target eligibility refreshes between hits when a Paladin dies.

Boons and afflictions share `StatusEffect`. Enemy attributes apply only when an attack removes player HP; shield, invincibility and invisibility block new afflictions as usual. Fireball adds one burning stack; High Priest elemental strikes apply two stacks of freeze, burn, stun, darkness or corrosion (never plague). Burning remains capped at ten, ticks for five damage per stack after the enemy phase, then loses one stack. Players break each frozen turn with three taps; stun affects the next accepted throw. The city remains immune to statuses.

## Debug dice

Open the **three-bar menu → Debug** to choose dice or load a wave by typing **1–20** and tapping **LOAD** (or pressing Enter). Loading starts a fresh run at that wave, with its matching full player health, size and enemy formation; wave 20 is the 20,000-HP city. It clears prior skills, statuses, treasure, combo, timers and animations. Invalid numbers leave the current run unchanged. The persistent Perfect toggle keeps its setting.

Open **Menu → Debug**, choose an illustrated double/triple or **Random Singles**, then charge and throw normally. Singles always have three different faces; doubles randomize the spare die. **Perfect: On** is a persistent toggle guaranteeing a Perfect on every accepted throw until switched off, snaps the marker to the center, and counts toward treasure. It can be combined with any selected result. The chosen dice combination applies once unless **Repeat** is on. Perfect remains on independently of Repeat, including after restart; use its toggle to turn it off. Canceled charging consumes neither setting. **Natural Random** restores power-weighted rolls, with normal Perfect odds if its toggle is on. Explicit combinations override the odds even on a Perfect stop.

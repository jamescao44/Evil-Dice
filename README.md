# Evil Dice

A Godot 4 pixel-art dice game: grow an eldritch horror through **nine randomized normal/elite waves → castle-city fever**, earning treasure through Perfect throws.

The shared [Chinese Google Design Doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1i-PEouZmzwagpz6CTa7CjMVl_UJd99-io_uB7TMgguM/edit) is the primary record for game designs and changes. Write future design updates there in Mandarin (Simplified Chinese), keeping proposals separate from implemented features. The earlier English Google Doc and `docs/game-design.md` remain reference snapshots.

## Language

The interface defaults to 简体中文. Open **Settings** on the home screen, or the three-bar menu in battle, to choose **简体中文** or **English**. Changes apply immediately and are saved locally, including browser storage in web builds.

Translations live in `assets/localization/ui.csv`. During local play in Godot, saving the CSV updates the running game in about a second—no manual reimport or restart. Keep the `keys` column and formatting placeholders unchanged. Invalid or incomplete saves keep the last valid translations and report the problem in Godot's output. Exported builds still bundle translations at build time; updating a published web game requires a new export/upload. See `docs/code-architecture.md` for implementation details.

## Play

Open `project.godot` in Godot and click **Run Project** for the portrait home screen. Open `scenes/home.tscn` and use **Run Current Scene** for the landscape home screen. The original `scenes/battle.tscn` and `scenes/battle_portrait.tscn` remain available for direct battle testing. Gameplay uses clicks or taps, with no keyboard controls.

The home screen has five bottom tabs, ordered **Dices / Ancients / Play / Summon / Shop**, with Play selected initially. Ancients shows The Horror, its lore and play style, and a skill reference for normal rolls, all doubles and all triples, including city variants. Swipe its portrait horizontally, drag with a mouse, or open the visual selection menu to browse the collection. Only The Horror is available; the carousel stays on it until more entries exist. Dices contains the basic Bone Dice, with a larger preview and descriptions on tap. The Play button displays the selected dice and starts a new ten-stage run in the same layout. Home in the battle grimoire or ending screen leaves the run and returns to Play, retaining the current session's selections and sound/effects/speed settings. Summon is a placeholder; Shop is blank. Rubies and summon shards exist only within the active run; there is no summoning, purchasing, or saved collection progression yet.

1. Click or tap inside the circular magic seal to start charging, then again to lock the marker and throw. The marker moves fastest near the center, using the gentler blended timing curve at another **10% slower overall speed** (about 2.667 seconds per round trip). The visible bar is 25% shorter: 216 landscape / 432 portrait logical pixels. Higher power increases combination odds and dice motion and sound. A **Perfect** within **±30 ms** of crossing the center, measured from the actual stop-input time guarantees a pair or triple: 50% each in squads, 100% triple in fever. Non-Perfect triples cap at 25% in squads; exact doubles cap at 70% in both modes after fever normalization. There are no pity guarantees. The marker moves continuously while charging; only a successful Perfect stop snaps it to the center and flashes the marker and bar white. Both layouts share the same time window regardless of frame rate.
2. Revealed dice form a **skill bubble**, rise enlarged to the middle, then settle lower and bob with their name and description. The used dice seal turns gray and its combination lines disappear. Dice remain visible and dim through held skills, casts, enemy turns and transitions, then brighten when ready; menus dim them further instead of hiding them. **In squad waves, drag skills onto the battlefield to cast.** Normal attacks, 11 and 111 must land on a foe, or can be tapped first and then aimed by tapping a foe. City rolls cast automatically after their reveal; saved targeted skills still select the city. Buffs, area skills and combos do not cast on a simple tap. Dragging a buff dims enemies while the Horror stays bright; attack selection and every cast keep characters fully lit. Dragging has no tether line or cancel hint; casting bubbles become see-through at 28% opacity with no dark fill. Invalid drops preserve the skill. **55 automatically delivers three hits; 555 delivers six after one field drop.** Each hit pops a dot and independently chooses a random living foe (the city in fever); remaining hits continue against bodies after all living foes are defeated. Cast bubbles stay where released and dissolve into pixel particles when finished. Enemy turns, boon clocks, the next roll and treasure claims wait for the whole action.
3. Every living enemy acts. Before acting, enemies show a sword (attack), plus (heal), shield, or boost-arrow intent icon. Hold an enemy for 0.25 seconds to inspect its move, buffs and status effects while the rest of the screen dims; release to dismiss. A short tap still casts a selected targeted skill, while holding never spends it. Dead bodies stay until the whole squad is defeated.
4. The Horror feeds, the bodies fade, and the scenery pans and shrinks as you advance and grow. Adventurers become progressively smaller across nine squad waves, leading into the city finale.
5. Stage ten is a circular castle city with a moat, bridges, dense houses, and a towering keep. Animation speed is fixed at 2×, with no frenzy bar or hold/spam speed boost. Pair and triple probabilities receive a 1.5× fever boost (non-Perfect triple ceiling 37.5%); if their total exceeds 100%, both are normalized proportionally. Perfect guarantees a triple. Every throw still needs a charge click and a stop click; holding never automatically releases dice. Each newly rolled skill automatically casts when its bubble finishes forming, including support and all combo hits; no aiming or field drop is needed. Rolls are still started and stopped manually, and saved bonus skills remain manual. Troops keep attacking while you charge and during animations outside cast pauses. They wiggle as they run, and civilians and aristocrats flee from destroyed houses inside the walls.

Top controls: ordinary animation speed, sound, and a **three-bar menu**. The menu page contains **Debug**, **Effects: Full/Reduced**, **Help / Grimoire**, and **Resume**; Help and Debug return to this page when closed. Active statuses appear as small independent icons directly above the Horror, each with its own remaining turns, burning stacks, next-throw stun count, or city-immunity seconds. Inactive or expired effects disappear individually. Regeneration, both attack boons, invincibility, freeze, burning, stun, darkness, corrosion, and invisibility use separate indicators; both attack boons add their bonuses while active. Hover for explanations or tap in portrait. Help includes a dedicated status page with concise descriptions of all eight combat statuses. The menu page, Help and Debug pause the charge meter, city attacks, charging audio and skill effects. Losing window focus cancels an unfinished charge without spending a roll. Restart after victory or defeat begins a new run.

The **SAVE slot** above and to the right of the dice stores one skill. Dragging a new skill into the empty slot ends the current turn without applying it. Later, drag the stored skill out for a bonus cast between rolls or while holding a new skill; targeted stored skills also support tap-then-select. The saved seal expands to full active size. Any waiting skill stays visible but dimmed and unavailable until the bonus finishes. Bonus casts do not add an enemy phase or tick existing boon durations; the subsequent ordinary action still does. A saved one-turn immunity protects the upcoming squad enemy phase. If a bonus cast clears the wave, an unused summoned skill automatically fills the vacated slot. Saved skills carry between waves, use current damage stats when cast, and reset with a new run. A full slot rejects replacement. The saved skill stays motionless and dimmed whenever interaction is locked, then resumes movement when usable.

## Combat and growth

Every rolled or saved skill is a `MultiHitSkill` object. Normal attacks, aimed attacks, area attacks, support and ultimates start with **one use**, hiding the use orbs; 55 starts with three and 555 with six. Future use multipliers apply to the entire skill: **×2 makes a regular skill two uses/two orbs, and Sixfold Ruin twelve hits/twelve orbs**. Damage previews, orb pops and combat all use the same count. Aimed skills keep their chosen living target and retarget after a kill; random combos choose independently each hit. Area and support skills repeat their whole effect, retaining the existing buff-refresh and healing limits. The full chain remains one action; it spends remaining hits on bodies before victory, and stops on player defeat.

For future modifiers, call `model.set_skill_use_multiplier("echo_buff", 2)` for all skills, or `skill.set_use_multiplier("relic_echo", 2)` for one skill object. Passing `1` removes that named source; refreshing the same source replaces it, while different sources multiply together. Saved skills preserve their own modifiers and use current model modifiers when cast. The use budget freezes at cast start so an expiring modifier cannot change the remaining hits or orb count midway. New runs clear model modifiers. Twin Scythes and Cursed Bow now supply ordered combo-use modifiers through this shared budget.

Normal base damage is **1.5 × sum of three different dice + flat relic damage**, with the existing normal per-hit caps. Skills use **regular attack power P = 15 + flat relic damage** and ignore caps. Let `s` be the spare die. Every numeric player hit is **floor(base damage × DMG)**, then normal caps, shields and remaining HP apply. **DMG starts at 1 + active boon bonuses + 0.1 × Perfect combo**, then applies relic modifiers in inventory order, followed by darkness. The natural baseline remains **1.0 in every wave, including the city**. Might adds +0.5, and Ascension adds +2.0; both active boons add together. The white damage multiplier at the battlefield’s top left stays hidden until a numerical attack is cast. It starts at `DMG (regular base) x 1.0`, changes to the skill base, applies bonuses and relics left to right, then combines into final damage. The entire expression waves; damage above 100 trembles with log10 scaling. It fades after the attack. Relics glow and tilt as larger, shiny versions of the multi-hit orbs strike the number; increases bob in a wave and decreases shake. Criticals are included in the cast display; flat-base and extra-hit relics react without changing DMG. Support skills and instant kills keep it hidden. Reduced effects keep the values and glow but suppress motion and particles. The former 1.5× starting multiplier is baked into base damage, including spare-die contributions; multiplier bonuses now apply relative to the 1.0 starting baseline. City 666 uses its explicit 2000 base instead. Keep fractional bases until the final hit is rounded down.

| Face | Double | Triple |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | One hit: 1.5P + 1.5s | Instantly kill one chosen foe; city: 5P base damage |
| 2 | Heal 5 at each of the next s turn starts | Restore full health, including grown maximum HP |
| 3 | +0.5 DMG for the next s actions | +2.0 DMG for the next 3 actions |
| 4 | Hit every foe for ⅔P + 1.5s | Hit every foe for 2P |
| 5 | Three automatic hits, each P + 1.5s; independently random foes | Six automatic hits, each P; independently random foes |
| 6 | Invincible for s enemy phases; city: 2 seconds per stack | **Annihilation**: clear squad; city: clear troops and deal 2000 × DMG. Heal 12 |

Both attack boons contribute while active; their +0.5 and +2.0 add to DMG rather than replacing or multiplying one another. Reapplying regeneration pays out all unpaid healing before refreshing it. Reapplying the same attack boon emits a 10-base (33) or 50-base (333) damage shockwave using current modifiers, then refreshes it. Invincibility adds its new duration to the time remaining. A squad turn ends after every surviving enemy finishes its skill: used buff counters decrease together at that point, never after the player alone or between combo hits. Attack boons retain their advertised subsequent actions (including support actions); regeneration heals at the next turn starts and counts down after those enemy phases. Invincibility counts the granting enemy phase and stays active through the last enemy's attack. Clearing a wave closes the turn immediately. In the city, regeneration and attack boons still count completed roll intervals; immunity instead lasts **2 real-time seconds per stack**. Its countdown continues while idle, charging, or casting, including cinematic pauses and slow motion, and pauses with menus/rewards. Reapplying adds duration without resetting a partially elapsed stack; ordinary or saved casts do not directly subtract a stack. Bonus casts do not advance the turn. Unspent durations carry between waves. Multi-hit actions finish their remaining hits against corpses after the last living foe dies.

Held attack skills show live per-hit damage as **base + modifier (xN)**; targeted skills show it when selected or dragged. For example, a 5/5/1 without bonuses reads `16 + 0 (x3)`. The modifier includes the global multiplier, relics, attack boons, Perfect combo, critical charges, darkness, corrosion and normal target caps. Base is the integer part of the skill base; the final sum matches combat's rounded impact damage before squad shields and remaining HP. Hovering a target selects its cap; before choosing, differing caps show a modifier range. Cap reductions can produce a negative modifier. Saved previews use current stats. This is an extra red line above the bubble; the original attack description stays below it. Squad instant kills have no numeric line; city 111 and 666 remain numerical. Regeneration shows green `5 HP (xN)` and full heal shows the actual missing health as `+N HP`. Portrait skills settle slightly lower to leave room between this line and the treasure bank. Turn-based buff captions use “for N turns”; city immunity uses “for N seconds”. An active matching boon adds a short gold reapply-bonus line beneath the regular skill description.

After each squad victory, gain 30 maximum HP and heal 55 up to the new maximum. There is no automatic damage-multiplier increase between waves. Normal caps are ⅓ HP in stages 1–2, ½ in stage 3, and ¾ from stage 4 onward. Growth is fixed, with no mutation choices. Visual Horror size now follows 224 / 248 / 260 / 272 / 280 / 288 / 296 / 308 / 320 / 288 logical pixels: stages 3–7 grow more gradually, and the city Horror is 10% smaller than stage nine without reducing its combat stats. Enemy sizes are 192 / 96 / 100 / 84 / 86 / 76 / 64 / 54 / 44 / 20: fights 5–9 have a further modest size increase. Enemies form compact groups through the battlefield: landscape groups shift inward, while portrait groups use the upper center and open right flank, reserving room for the Horror and status icons. Health bars, intent icons and target areas remain separate.

The city has 10,000 HP and the same 1.0 damage baseline as squad waves. **666 deals 2000 × the full DMG multiplier**, limited by remaining city HP, using the same multiplier as other numeric attacks. At ×1.0, city 666 deals 2000 damage before flat relic bonuses. City 111 deals 5× regular attack power, not an instant city kill. AoE clears all troops; each rapid hit removes at most one troop. Heal and power-up triples do no damage and do not clear troops. Invincibility blocks both troop and ranged damage; city timers and reinforcements continue. Buildings and walls turn to rubble as health falls.

## Perfect combo and treasure

A **Perfect combo** counter sits opposite the saved skill slot, showing the consecutive Perfect count. The total damage multiplier is shown separately in white at the battlefield’s top left. Each accepted Perfect adds one Combo and **+0.1 to DMG**, without a cap; the bonus adds alongside treasure and both attack boons. The bonus applies to the skill from that throw and every subsequent hit, including saved skills at their current cast-time stats and city Annihilation. A non-Perfect throw resets it to zero. Canceling charge, using saved skills, claiming treasure, breaking ice, and changing waves preserve the streak; restarting clears it. The multiplier applies before integer rounding and normal damage caps. Healing and instant kills retain their existing behavior. Debug Perfects count once.

The treasure bank shows nine diamonds without a heading. Perfect throws fill **nine diamond slots**, preserved through misses and waves. The bank stops at nine until claimed. **3 Perfects = one reward; 6 = a rare chest with three rewards; 9 = an epic chest with five rewards.** This replaces the old random 25% three-reward bonus. The **third, sixth and ninth diamonds** are larger when filled. Only the highest available chest has a strong pulsing halo and a bright, clickable **CLAIM** label; lower tiers cannot be claimed. Claiming empties the entire meter, including extra diamonds. There is no separate chest button. Claims are disabled while a skill forms, waits, targets or casts, including pauses between multi-hit casts. The whole skill must finish before rewards can boost subsequent actions. Final city victory automatically claims the highest available chest and locks its one/three/five rewards. A brief “Common/Rare/Epic chest auto claimed” banner overlays the success screen for 2.4 real-time seconds. The normal chest-opening and reward-reel animation then plays automatically, and **each gift waits for the player to tap and collect it**. Rewards apply once on pickup; after the last gift’s effect, the success screen returns. Incomplete banks show no claim message. Squad waves preserve the bank; defeat retains manual claims.

The model selects every reward before animation: **50% relic**, **25% rubies**, and **25% summon shard**. A relic reward first rolls **58% common / 30% rare / 10% epic / 1% legendary / 1% cursed**, then picks uniformly among eligible relics in that rarity. Cursed is not a separate reward category roll. Repeated categories are allowed, but concrete relic prizes are distinct, unowned and non-fusion. Empty rarity pools redistribute proportionally; if no eligible relics remain, the gift becomes rubies. Reels use separate cosmetic RNG and include every obtainable reward, scrolling actual relic icons and land on the already locked reward. A 0.38-second pause and white flash precede the chest opening and throwing loot up into extended slot reels. Five rewards use a 3+2 layout on phones and a row in landscape. **All rewards wait for individual taps after every reel stops, including the automatically claimed victory chest.** Each tap applies that gift once, shows its skill-style name, and plays its category effect. **SKIP REMAINING** is available during the reels and after any number of pickups, including full-inventory replacement. It keeps collected gifts, discards the rest without compensation, and immediately returns to combat or the success screen. Combat and city timers pause while treasure is open. One relic reward slot becomes a tarot draw, using the same three-card selection as wave rewards. Rubies rain; curses glow ominously; relics shine. Flat damage, multiplier upgrades, and potion placeholders no longer drop or appear in the reels. Reduced effects suppresses flash, shake, and particle bursts.

| Reward | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Blood Rubies | +100 run rubies; ruby icon and rain replace coins |
| Relic | Roll rarity first, then pick an eligible relic uniformly within it; includes cursed relics |
| Summon Shard | +1 run shard, represented by a parchment contract with a magic circle |

Ruby and shard counters appear in the header. New runs clear both currencies, the Perfect bank, rewards, and bonuses; no persistent economy or summoning is implemented. Instant kills remain unscaled; city Annihilation uses the full DMG multiplier. Debug Perfects count.

Dice use weighted category and face selection. Triple faces 1–5 each have weight **1**, and 666 has weight **0.7** (about 12.28% of triple results). This preserves the total triple probability: regular Perfects remain 50% triple / 50% pair; city Perfects retain guaranteed triples. Non-Perfect caps remain 25% triples (up to 37.5% in fever) and 70% pairs. Per-run category, pair, triple, single-face, and reward weights remain explicit hooks. Lucky Die adds five percentage points to the chance of a roll containing a six; Cursed Die and Cursed Stopwatch modify accepted rolls in inventory order, with the stopwatch always forbidding 666. Zero-power category probabilities retain their old values, but triple faces are now weighted.


Damage popups show the calculated hit before the remaining-health clamp, including lethal hits, burning, and Prismatic strikes. Actual HP loss and lifesteal remain capped by remaining HP. Squad instant kills show impact effects without damage numbers; city 111 and 666 remain numeric attacks. Number size is `24 × max(1, log10(damage))`, rounded to a whole pixel size, so 10 / 100 / 1,000 damage use 24 / 48 / 72. Other feedback keeps its regular size.

## Tarot

The 22 tarot cards are single-use consumables in a separate, unlimited inventory with duplicates. Their effects can change rolls, react to roll outcomes, modify the next skill, or apply immediately. Tap the card icon to switch the dice area to the swipeable carousel; tap a card to inspect or drag it onto the battlefield to use it. The battlefield keeps running. Reward draws shuffle a deck from right to left, then fan it out; pick three, reveal them, and keep one. Drawn cards bob gently with staggered phases and remain still with reduced effects.

## Relics

Every cleared wave, including the city, offers two distinct relics and a tarot draw. Each relic choice rolls **58% common / 30% rare / 10% epic / 1% legendary / 1% cursed**, then picks uniformly within that rarity, excluding owned unique relics and fusion-only results. These are total probabilities per rarity, independent of the number of relics it contains. Empty rarity pools are removed and the remaining weights renormalized. Choose one, or skip for **+10 rubies**. A Game Coin held before clearing also grants +10 rubies. The offer locks until resolved and cannot be rerolled by reopening it.

The inventory holds up to ten relics outside the shortened battlefield, below the raised player health bar. Sourced fantasy sprites sit on one translucent rounded bar, without visible cells or slot numbers. Hold a relic to show its description, including counts and stored charges; releasing dismisses it. The held icon enlarges slightly, moves only horizontally, and a grey rounded square marks its destination while neighbors preview the order. Small bottom-right counters show page stacks, stored charges, Reaper kills, and Cursed Bow extra hits. Charged Magic Shield and Magic Sword repeat a gold claim-style glow until their last stored charge is spent; reduced effects keeps a steady glow. Effects resolve **left to right**. With ten occupied slots, selecting a new relic asks which existing relic to replace. Chest pickups use the same replacement flow, with an option to leave that gift behind (no skip rubies). Picking a relic plays a short lift-and-slam into its inventory position, with a bounce, impact ring and landing sound; stacks, replacements and fusions share the animation. Rewards commit once on selection, and progression waits for the landing. Codex Pages share one slot; the tenth fuses into Ancient Codex. All six magic stones fuse into Prismatic Stone at the earliest component position. Both fusion relics are excluded from drops, and their components stop appearing once the fused relic is owned. Inventory and relic counters reset on a new run.

All **29 relics** in the current design are implemented. Flat damage, health, armor, lifesteal, immunity, ordered damage modifiers, combo-hit modifiers and roll modifiers feed the shared combat model. Magic Shield and Magic Sword count six completed turns across waves, then store a whole-hit block or a ×3 critical for the next attack skill and all its hits. Saved bonus casts do not advance these counters. Last Contract prevents one death and restores 60% max HP. Reaper's Scythe counts enemy kills but excludes city troops. Prismatic Stone combines all six stones and adds a 100-base-damage strike on one random foe for each Perfect. Fortune Cookie grants a random tarot on acquisition and every three completed turns; Crystal Ball grants one each completed turn. Both respect tarot bans and carry counters across waves. Ancient Prophecy activates each used tarot twice, with fresh choices/targets when needed and the existing stacking/overwrite rules. The repeat cannot recursively repeat, even if Fool replaces the relic. A second impossible effect is skipped; the player may decline a repeat requiring input. Emperor/World resolve and lock each eligible reward before its picker.

| Status | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Burning | 5 damage per stack after the enemy phase; capped at 10; loses 1 stack per turn |
| Freeze | Player breaks ice with 3 taps, ending the turn; enemies skip one action per freeze stack |
| Stun | Scrambles the next player throw; enemies cannot be stunned |
| Darkness | Halves outgoing damage and blocks healing; 3 stacks per application, loses 1 per turn |
| Corrosion | Disables defenses and increases incoming damage by 50%; 3 stacks per application, loses 1 per turn |
| Plague | Reduces max HP by 10 per stack to a minimum of 1; grows by 1 and spreads 1 stack to a random living ally per turn; persists |

Runestone adds one application stack to subsequent status relics in inventory order. Enemy status icons appear below their health bars, enemy buffs (shield and next-attack boost) appear alongside ailments in hold inspection. Breaking a shield immobilizes its owner for one enemy action; corrosion or execution bypasses do not count as breaks. Freeze and shield-break locks overlap on the same skipped action. Player plague is disabled. Player statuses carry into the city, whose own health target remains immune.

## Ten waves and enemy traits

With `n` = normal and `e` = elite: **1n → 3n → 1e → 5n → 2n1e → 3n2e → 5n2e → 4e → 5n5e → city**. Roles are sampled independently and shuffled; repeated roles are allowed. Enemy base stats do not increase with the wave. Enemy names are hidden. Health bars shrink to fit the actual column spacing, with room for their borders; crowded waves retain separate rows. Elites have an attribute-colored silhouette, bright foot rings, and rising cylindrical light columns: gold stun, orange fire, and ice blue. Reduced effects retains static rings and columns.

| Role | Normal HP / attack | Elite HP / attack | Support actions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vanguard | 75 / 5 | 150 / 10 | Self-shield +6, capped at 20 |
| Mage | 35 / 10 | 75 / 15 | Self-heal 30% max HP; elite can give one other unbuffed ally ×1.5 on its next attack |
| Elf Archer | 50 / 7.5 | 100 / 10 | Elite heals all living allies, including itself, for 20% of their own max HP |

Every living enemy chooses one action per round. When useful support is available, vanguards/elite archers attack 70% of the time and mages attack 65%; otherwise they attack. Healing cannot resurrect or exceed max HP. Mage buffs do not stack and remain until the recipient attacks. Damage/healing round once to integer HP (a normal archer deals 8). All elites start with **10 shield**. A shield absorbs an entire hit even when that hit breaks it: no excess reaches health. Later hits in a combo can hit HP. Triple 1 and squad Annihilation ignore shields. Elites reuse the sprites with gold vanguard, orange mage, and ice-blue archer glows and health bars.

## Elite attributes and statuses

Boons and afflictions share `StatusEffect` objects. The next move, including its attribute, is chosen once at round start; inspecting it never rerolls it. Attributes apply only when an enemy attack actually removes HP: invincibility and fully absorbed hits block new afflictions. Normal enemies have no attributes. Magic-stone relics now add player attack attributes when a hit removes enemy HP; the city is immune to all statuses.

- **Elite archer:** 50% of attack choices are ice arrows. Freeze locks rolls and saved skills, encases the motionless Horror and dice area in ice, and waits for **three taps** on either ice shell. Each tap cracks it; the third breaks free and ends the player's turn, followed by the enemy phase. It never automatically times out. When the player is already frozen at intent selection, every elite archer schedules an ordinary arrow instead of an ice arrow. Breaking free therefore guarantees a playable following turn; ice arrows can be assigned again during that turn.
- **Elite mage:** every attack that removes HP adds **2 burning stacks**, capped at **10**. At nine stacks only one is added; at ten no extra stack or popup is added. After all enemies act, each stack deals five HP in consecutive `-5` ticks, then one stack expires. Death stops the sequence. Invincibility blocks the damage while stacks still decay. Carried burning ticks after a complete ordinary city action; bonus skills do not trigger extra ticks.
- **Elite vanguard:** 50% of attack choices stun. Stars circle above the Horror; the next accepted throw uses an erratic power marker with random direction and speed. Canceling charge or breaking ice preserves the pending stun. Accepted throws consume it, and cosmetic motion never changes combat RNG.

## Debug dice

Open the **three-bar menu → Debug** to choose dice or load a wave by typing **1–10** and tapping **LOAD** (or pressing Enter). Loading starts a fresh run at that wave, with its matching full player health, size and enemy formation; wave 10 is the 10,000-HP city. It clears prior skills, statuses, treasure, combo, timers and animations. Invalid numbers leave the current run unchanged. The persistent Perfect toggle keeps its setting.

Open **Menu → Debug**, choose an illustrated double/triple or **Random Singles**, then charge and throw normally. Singles always have three different faces; doubles randomize the spare die. **Perfect: On** is a persistent toggle guaranteeing a Perfect on every accepted throw until switched off, snaps the marker to the center, and counts toward treasure. It can be combined with any selected result. The chosen dice combination applies once unless **Repeat** is on. Perfect remains on independently of Repeat, including after restart; use its toggle to turn it off. Canceled charging consumes neither setting. **Natural Random** restores power-weighted rolls, with normal Perfect odds if its toggle is on. Explicit combinations override the odds even on a Perfect stop.

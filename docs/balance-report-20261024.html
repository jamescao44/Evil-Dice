Evil Dice · 24 October 2026 · three completed revisions

Later design update: Mithril Blade now grants a ×2 damage multiplier (+100%), replacing +25 flat base. The benchmark results below are preserved from the completed three-pass study and used the previous +25 version.

Shorter pressure peaks.
Room to feel stronger.

Enemy growth now separates durability, damage and XP. Boss health is lower, dangerous late enemy attacks are gentler, and four runaway relic values are bounded. Player base damage and its +10% per-level growth are unchanged.

Why this approach

Valve’s Left 4 Dead presentation describes alternating buildup, peaks and recovery rather than unchanging pressure. I used that pacing principle as a design analogy, not its real-time durations or adaptive system. Evil Dice now uses a fixed wave curve that does not secretly react to equipment or player performance. Valve, pp. 77–81 and 91.

GameFlow treats challenge alongside control, goals and feedback, so I preserved visible leveling, big skill payoffs and boss interruption. A later preregistered study with 311 participants found no significant enjoyment difference from difficulty–skill ratios alone. Consequently, these tests measure mechanical pacing—not boredom, frustration, dopamine or proven enjoyment. Sweetser & Wyeth, 2005; Cutting et al., 2023.

Three actual test-and-revise passes

1. Changed enemy progression, special-unit damage and boss health, and increased enemy XP. The first-boss representative median fell from 30 to 16 rolls; wave 19 no longer abruptly killed four of twelve representative builds.
2. Reduced the four relic outliers after isolated-item tests exposed their contribution. Single-item late-wave cases became meaningfully weaker, while multi-item synergies still paid off.
3. Added post-boss relief, strengthened the late pressure beat, scaled soul chests, and reduced Commander base HP further. Its final median was 16.5 rolls: lower HP did not guarantee fewer rolls because phase boundaries, guards and random outcomes also affect pacing.

Evidence, scope and remaining risk

Each version ran 1,056 encounter probes plus 12 natural runs: 4,224 probes and 48 runs, followed by 36 fresh-seed runs. The shared Godot CLI adapter resolves real combat, status, tarot, loot and shop rules. The fixed policy uses visible previews, chooses damage/kill opportunities, values charge interruption, takes utility tarots, buys ranked relics and chooses routes. It does not optimize every tarot (notably Magician), reorder items optimally, or model touch mistakes. Isolated probes use full health, no tarots, an authored representative inventory, and combat-only XP from prior rosters without shop skips.

Reroll Treasure explicitly claims Greed. Randomly upgraded Ultra wave-chest prizes are discarded; their automatic opening currency is still paid. Lower chests, shop and tarot rolls can naturally supply legendary relics; their rarity pool was not rebalanced. The full-run reward/RNG paths diverge after combat changes, so only controlled probes isolate loadouts reliably.

Remaining concern: passive roll-and-heal play can stall bosses for a long time. The final Dragon-only no-skill probes can still survive some very long fights through leftover healing. I did not compensate with lethal unavoidable attacks. Boss pressure and healing timing deserve the next GUI playtest. CLI results cannot establish animation pacing, touch feel, FPS, audio quality or whether a fight feels boring.

Targeted regression checks cover independent scales, phase/guard inheritance, XP receipt sizes, capped Reaper retriggers, pets, Greed selection, enemy actions, relics, localization and CLI parity. The existing CLI regression suite still emits shutdown resource-leak warnings after passing; the balance probe batches finish without errors. One baseline passive Dragon case reached the 600-command safety limit (405 rolls) and is retained as censored, not a clear. Final probes had no capped cases.

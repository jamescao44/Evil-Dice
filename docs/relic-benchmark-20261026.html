What changed

  • Healing conversion is the standout. Replacing Last Contract with Steel Sword, copied by Disguise Mask, raises the old healing build from 22k to 187k single-target damage. This uses existing relics and gives up Contract’s death prevention. Holy Grail instead of Book of the Dead also reaches 188k, but loses the tripled HP. The old benchmark understated this build.
  • Generic multipliers still drive item builds. Pet Ghost → Steel Sword raises the adjusted item build from 26k to 66k; Ticket then raises it to 77k. A dedicated bone/Grail/Ticket version reaches 60k single / 74k group. The new item curse creates an option, but does not beat the tested Dragon-and-Mask setup.
  • Magic Banana improves raw damage around 22% versus Last Contract, trading survival insurance for damage and disappearance risk. Black Chip improves one-roll damage around 21%. Red Chip helps group damage more than Blue Chip in these tests.
  • Status and single-target gains are small. Potion + Hellfire changes the status result by roughly 2–4%; Ticket’s single-target build gain is roughly 3–5% with substantially more rolls. These small differences should not be treated as decisive. Burst improves, but its largest single-target turn averages only 12k versus raw’s 34k.

Potion X has a useful niche: fast ordinary-enemy clearing, while Mithril Blade wins the final-boss comparison. Wave 24 starts at player LV.20 and wave 30 at LV.27, based on prior enemy XP. Failed runs are excluded from clear-time medians.

Witch fusion can reduce damage. With Slingshot, Steel Sword and Holy Grail, five bones deal 35.5k single-target damage over ten turns; fusion gives 34.3k. The five attacks total 35 + 5 × flat bonus, versus Witch’s 50 + flat bonus. A flat bonus above 3.75 favors the separate attacks, although fusion frees slots. This supplemental comparison has different acquisition costs and is excluded from the equal-budget table.
